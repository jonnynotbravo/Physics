# Thermodynamics

**Thermodynamics** is the study of heat, temperature, and energy flow. It explains why things get hot or cold, how engines work, and why some processes are irreversible.

---

## Heat vs. Temperature

Heat and temperature are **NOT the same thing** — this is one of the most common confusions in physics.

---

## What is Temperature?

**Temperature** = a measure of the **average kinetic energy** of particles in a substance.

### What This Means:
- Temperature measures **how fast particles are moving** (on average)
- Higher temperature = particles moving **faster** (hot)
- Lower temperature = particles moving **slower** (cold)
- Temperature is a **property** of an object (like mass or color)
- Temperature tells you **how hot or cold** something is

### Units:
- **Celsius (°C)** — water freezes at 0°C, boils at 100°C
- **Kelvin (K)** — absolute scale starting at absolute zero
  - Conversion: \( K = °C + 273.15 \)
  - 0 K = absolute zero
  - 273.15 K = 0°C (water freezes)
  - 373.15 K = 100°C (water boils)
- **Fahrenheit (°F)** — used in US
  - Water freezes at 32°F, boils at 212°F

---

## What is Heat?

**Heat** = **energy transfer** between objects due to a temperature difference.

### What This Means:
- Heat is **NOT** a property of an object (you don't "have heat")
- Heat is **energy in motion** — transferring from one object to another
- Heat is a **process**, not a thing
- Heat **only flows** when there's a temperature difference
- Heat **always flows** from higher temperature → lower temperature (automatically)

### Unit:
- **Joule (J)** — same unit as energy
- Also: **calorie (cal)** — 1 cal = 4.184 J
  - Food "Calories" (capital C) = kilocalories = 1000 cal

---

## The Key Difference:

| Temperature | Heat |
|------------|------|
| **Property** of an object | **Process** (energy transfer) |
| Measures average particle kinetic energy | Energy moving between objects |
| "How hot or cold something is" | Energy flowing from hot → cold |
| Can measure at any moment | Only exists during transfer |
| Units: °C, K, °F | Units: Joules (J) |
| Examples: "Water is 50°C" | Examples: "100 J of heat flowed" |

---

## Analogy:

Think of it like money:

- **Temperature** = how much money you have in your account (a property you have)
- **Heat** = money being transferred from one account to another (a process)

You don't "have heat" — heat is what happens when energy moves.

Similarly:
- **Temperature** = "height" (a property)
- **Heat** = "falling" (a process)

You can't say "I have falling" — you can only say "I am falling."

---

## Example 1: Hot Water in Fridge

**Before:**
- Hot water: 80°C (particles moving fast)
- Fridge air: 4°C (particles moving slowly)
- **Temperature difference exists**

**What happens:**
- Energy flows from hot water → cold air
- **This energy flow = HEAT**
- Water particles slow down → water temperature decreases
- Air particles speed up → air temperature increases (slightly)

**After (equilibrium):**
- Water: 4°C (same as fridge)
- Fridge air: 4°C
- **No temperature difference → heat flow stops**

**Heat = the energy that MOVED from water to air**

---

## Example 2: Ice Cube in Boiling Water

**Before:**
- Ice cube: 0°C (particles moving slowly)
- Boiling water: 100°C (particles moving fast)
- **Big temperature difference**

**What happens:**
- **Heat flows** from hot water → cold ice
- Ice temperature increases (0°C → melting → warming)
- Water temperature decreases (100°C → slightly less)
- **Heat = energy flowing from water to ice**

**Eventually:**
- Both reach same temperature (thermal equilibrium)
- Heat flow stops

## Absolute Zero

**Absolute zero** = 0 Kelvin = -273.15°C = -459.67°F

### What is it?
- The **lowest possible temperature**
- At absolute zero, particles have **minimum possible kinetic energy**
- Particles essentially stop moving (quantum mechanics prevents complete stop, but thermal motion is minimal)
- **You cannot go below absolute zero**

### Why does it exist?
- Temperature = average kinetic energy of particles
- Kinetic energy cannot be negative
- Therefore, there's a minimum temperature: absolute zero

### Kelvin Scale:
- Starts at absolute zero (0 K)
- No negative temperatures
- Used in science because it's absolute (not arbitrary like Celsius or Fahrenheit)

**Examples:**
- 0 K = absolute zero (minimum temperature)
- 273.15 K = 0°C (water freezes)
- 373.15 K = 100°C (water boils)
- Room temperature ≈ 293 K (20°C)

---

## Direction of Heat Flow

**Heat ALWAYS flows from higher temperature to lower temperature.**

This is automatic and spontaneous:
- Hot object loses energy (cools down)
- Cold object gains energy (warms up)
- Continues until both reach same temperature (thermal equilibrium)

**You cannot make heat flow from cold to hot spontaneously** — that would require work (like a refrigerator or air conditioner, which use energy to pump heat "uphill").

---

## Thermal Equilibrium

**Thermal equilibrium** = when two objects reach the same temperature.

### What happens:
- Before equilibrium: temperature difference → heat flows
- At equilibrium: same temperature → heat flow stops
- Objects can still exchange energy, but **net heat flow = 0**

**Example:**
- Hot coffee + cold spoon
- Heat flows: coffee → spoon
- Eventually: both at same temperature
- Thermal equilibrium reached

---

## 📐 Important Concepts Summary:

**Temperature:**
- Measure of average particle kinetic energy
- Property of an object
- Units: °C, K, °F
- Fast particles = high temperature (hot)
- Slow particles = low temperature (cold)

**Heat:**
- Energy transfer due to temperature difference
- Process, not a property
- Units: Joules (J)
- Always flows from hot → cold
- Stops when thermal equilibrium reached

**Absolute Zero:**
- 0 K = -273.15°C
- Lowest possible temperature
- Particles have minimum kinetic energy

**Thermal Equilibrium:**
- Objects at same temperature
- No net heat flow
